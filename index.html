My name is Nathan

I was a SONAR technician whe I was in the Navy. My job mostly consisted of maintainence and drills, which took up about ninety percent of what I did during my time during my sea duty. the other ten percent of the time was when I got to put my skills to the test and operate on the watch floor in the real world. The drills and maintainence was quite monotonous, but those moments when it was me, four other operators and the supervisor standing behind me with blue overhead lights, green displays in front of me, and a high interest contact classified on the status board, to me, there was no where else I would rather be. The following is a quote I used to get a chuckle seeing every time I would go from SONAR Control to the Combat Information Center that was on a plaque sitting at the ASWE's console.Anti Subarine Warefare is a science of vague assumptions based upon debatable figures taken from inconclusive experiments performed with instruments of problematical accuracy by persons of doubtful reliability and questionable mentality.

A standard count test of the ships 1mc also known as the ships intercoms that the whole crew can hear is a count to ten followed by a count from ten to one. When the standard count test is being conducted, depending on the command, everyone is to stop what they are doing and listen for the duration of the test. This is why most comands do a short count test, which only counts to three and back..

When I was in the navy, I went on two deployments when I was serving onboard USS William P. Lawrence. We made several port calls to places like Guam, Australia, Singapore, Japan, Mexico, Guatemala, Equidor, Jamaica, Panama, Martinique France, and Cuba. We also got to visit California a few times and we were stationed out of Pearl Harbor Hawaii..
